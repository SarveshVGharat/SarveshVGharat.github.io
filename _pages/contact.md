## Contact Me

The best way to contact me is by email. Please use one of the following subject prefixes so that I can identify your query easily.

### General Queries — `[General]`

For general or professional queries, please use `[General]` followed by a short and specific subject line.

### Questions About My Papers — `[Paper Question]`

If you have a question about one of my papers, please mention the paper title and the specific section, theorem, equation, algorithm, or experiment related to your question. It would also help if you briefly explain what you understand and where the confusion arises.

### Research Collaborations — `[Research]`

I am always interested in research ideas with strong motivation and a clear problem formulation. If you would like to discuss a potential collaboration, please briefly explain:

- What problem do you want to study, and why is it important?
- Why are existing approaches insufficient?
- How do you propose to address the problem?
- What theoretical, empirical, or practical contribution do you expect?
- What preliminary evidence, experiments, or experimental plan supports the idea?
- What kind of collaboration are you looking for?

The idea does not need to be complete, but the email should present a concrete research problem rather than only a broad area of interest. I greatly value collaborators who are willing to think deeply, work consistently, and put sustained effort into the problem, and I hold myself to the same standard.

I believe that good research takes time and that papers are a secondary outcome of doing meaningful work. Therefore, if your primary goal is to complete and submit a paper within three or four months, I may not be the right person to collaborate with.

### Please Note

Since it has been quite some time since I prepared for GATE or began my own research journey, I may not be the right person to advise on GATE preparation, getting started with research, or general personal guidance.

I am also unable to provide arXiv endorsements unless the paper is closely related to my expertise or I can responsibly assess the work—for example, when it has been accepted at a recognized peer-reviewed venue.
